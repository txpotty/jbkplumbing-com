    Septic Lid and Riser Repair in Broken Arrow

    Old concrete lids crack, and a cracked lid over a 1,000-gallon tank is a real danger to kids and pets. We replace failing lids and add risers that bring access up to the lawn. Future pumping gets cheaper. Each lid we install gets screws or a lock so a child cannot lift it.

    Septic Tee Repair in Broken Arrow

    Sanitary tees on the inlet and outlet can crack or get knocked off by the hose on a pump truck. Without the outlet tee, the floating scum layer drifts into the laterals and plugs the gravel. We glue in a new Schedule 40 tee. An effluent filter goes on if the outlet has room.

    Effluent Pump Replacement in Broken Arrow, OK

    Effluent pumps in a dosing chamber last roughly 8 to 15 years, and they usually quit without much warning at all. A pump that hums but moves no water has a stuck impeller or a bad capacitor. We pull it and test the discharge line before setting a new one. Floats get replaced at the same time.

    Septic Maintenance and Small Repairs in Broken Arrow

    A sludge judge, a long clear tube, tells us how full the tank really is. Each maintenance call includes a sludge and scum reading, and we pump when the solids reach about a third of the tank depth. Filters get rinsed. Small cracks around the riser get sealed before groundwater finds them.

    Commercial Septic Repair in Broken Arrow, OK

    Churches and small offices on septic run more water through the tank than any house, and a commercial kitchen adds grease on top of that.     Inlet and Outlet Baffle Replacement in Broken Arrow, OK

    Baffles hold back the scum layer on top and the sludge on the bottom so only clear effluent leaves the tank. Concrete ones get eaten by sulfuric acid from sewer gas and eventually fall off into the tank. With no outlet baffle, sludge flows right out to the leach lines. We replace them with PVC sanitary tees. Usually a short job.

    Leach Field Restoration in Broken Arrow, OK

    Before we condemn a leach field, we open the distribution box and look at how evenly it splits the flow. A box that has settled or cracked can push all the effluent into a single trench. A new or leveled box brings a lot of fields back. Some fields are past saving. For those, we explain the design and permit steps.
