    Pilot Light and Burner Issues in Broken Arrow

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. No flame at all usually means a plugged pilot orifice or a bad piezo. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. Each part gets tested in that sequence.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Broken Arrow

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. A manometer reading tells us the supply pressure. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Broken Arrow

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. When one quits, it is usually the blower motor, the pressure switch or the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. We read the error code first. Then each part gets a meter test.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Broken Arrow, OK

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. The tech checks the dial, the immersion sensor and the outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part.
