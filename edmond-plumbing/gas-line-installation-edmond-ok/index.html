    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Installation in Edmond

    A simple indoor line for a range or dryer usually costs $350 to $1,000. Outdoor runs to a grill, fire pit or pool heater often run $1,000 to $3,500, depending on distance, trenching and line size.

    Black iron and CSST cover most of our work. Black iron is rigid and suits exposed runs, while CSST bends through finished walls with fewer joints and installs faster, so the choice usually comes down to where the pipe will run and how much wall you want opened. Both meet code.

    Most indoor appliance hookups take a few hours. Outdoor trenching projects usually take one to two days plus inspection.

    Bonding ties the CSST to the home's electrical grounding system to reduce lightning damage. It is required by code and the manufacturer, so every install gets it.

    Good gas lines rarely need scheduled inspections. Have them checked during routine plumbing visits, or right away if you notice an odd smell, a hissing sound or a spike in the gas bill. Homes built from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s may have polybutylene supply lines, which are known for sudden leaks. Older homes benefit from a look every 5 to 10 years.

    Common materials are black iron and CSST, with copper tubing used in a few places for small lines where local code allows it. We choose the material based on the application, along with local code. Buried lines use yellow polyethylene.

    For CSST we install Gastite and TracPipe, and for rigid work we use black iron from established manufacturers. Shutoff valves and fittings come from brands like Mueller and Nibco, chosen because they hold up and parts stay available. Quality valves last longer.

    A rotten egg smell is the main warning sign of a leak. A hissing sound near a pipe or a patch of dead grass over a buried line are others, and if you notice any of them, leave the building, call your gas provider from outside, and then call us. Do not try to find it yourself.
