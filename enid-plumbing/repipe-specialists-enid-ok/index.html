    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Enid

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Both still turn up in older homes. The pipe at the water heater usually tells us which one you have, and we replace the full system so the home is easier to insure and to sell.

    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Enid, OK

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. For a typical house, copper often runs 20 to 40 percent above PEX, though some buyers and homeowners like the look and feel of metal pipe and are willing to pay for it. Both prices include permits, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Enid

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. We use Uponor or similar PEX-A with cold expansion fittings that open the pipe end and let it shrink back over the fitting for a full-bore joint. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Two or three days covers most houses.

    Copper Repiping in Enid, OK

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. We install Type L copper, which has a thicker wall than the Type M used in many older homes, with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings. Expect a slightly longer job. Most copper jobs run a day longer than PEX, since each turn needs its own fitting.

    Repipe Costs Versus Leak Repairs in Enid, OK

    A whole-home PEX repipe for an average house often runs $5,000 to $12,000, with copper costing more. One slab leak with access and repair often costs $1,000 to $4,000. The numbers add up quickly. After two or three leaks, a repipe often pays for itself, and we give you a side-by-side comparison so you can make that call with real numbers in front of you.

    Commercial Repiping in Enid, OK

    Commercial buildings in Enid, OK often have larger pipe, risers that serve several floors and tenants who cannot lose water for a full day. Our crew breaks commercial repipes into zones, working one riser or suite at a time while the rest of the building keeps water. Night work is possible. Materials are usually Type L copper or commercial PEX, sized to the fixture count and installed to commercial code.

    Emergency Pipe Failures and Repipes in Enid

    Sometimes a house reaches the repipe point all at once, with a burst line and two more leaks right behind it.     Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Enid

    Most whole-home repipes in Enid, OK fall somewhere between $4,000 and $15,000. Bigger homes cost more. The final number depends on square footage, how many fixtures need new lines, whether you choose PEX or copper and how much drywall has to be opened and patched along the route. The quote comes in writing.

    We have to open some drywall, but the openings are small and planned. Most are about the size of a sheet of paper. We cut clean squares at fixtures and along the route, patch every one after the inspection and match the existing texture, so the walls are ready to paint when we finish.

    Whole-home repipes require a permit and inspection in most Oklahoma jurisdictions. We pull the permit and book the inspections.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. Uponor PEX-A is our usual PEX. Viega and Zurn PEX show up on some jobs, but we keep one brand throughout each house so the warranty holds.

    A repipe needs a permit, specialized tools, code knowledge and a passing pressure test. It takes skill and time. Hidden leaks, failed inspections or a voided pipe warranty from a DIY job can easily cost more than a licensed crew would have.

    Most repipes in Enid, OK take three to five working days for an average house. Big houses take more time. Copper takes a little longer than PEX because every turn needs a fitting, and the drywall patching usually adds a final day after the plumbing passes inspection.

    Copper is rigid metal pipe with a long track record. PEX bends instead. PEX is cheaper, quicker and quieter and handles freezes better, while copper tolerates sun and heat and suits owners who want metal, so it depends on your house.
