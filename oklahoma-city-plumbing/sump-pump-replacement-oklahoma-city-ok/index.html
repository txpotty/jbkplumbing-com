    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Oklahoma City

    The cost of a sump pump replacement in Oklahoma City, OK can range from $500 to $1,500, depending on the type of pump, its horsepower and the complexity of the installation. Factors like needing a new sump pit, major re-piping or adding a battery backup system can affect the final price. We provide transparent, upfront quotes after assessing your needs.

    Constant running, loud grinding, frequent cycling or failure to start are common signs. Rust, age and a burning smell also indicate trouble.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, powered by your home's electricity. A backup sump pump, usually battery-powered or water-powered, is a secondary system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or cannot keep up with heavy water inflow. Many Oklahoma City homes benefit from a backup system due to storm risks.

    We install Zoeller, Liberty and Wayne pumps. Cast iron models are our pick for durability.

    While it might seem like a simple task, sump pump replacement involves electrical connections, proper plumbing and making sure the pump is correctly sized and installed to prevent future flooding. DIY replacement can lead to improper operation, electrical hazards or system failure. It is best to hire a professional plumber for a safe and effective installation.

    Most sump pump replacements take one to two hours. Battery backups or new discharge lines take a bit longer.

    It is a good idea to test your sump pump at least every three months, and especially before and after rainy seasons. To test, simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit. The pump should activate, drain the water and then shut off automatically. If it does not, it is time for a professional inspection.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished living space below grade, a backup is a wise investment. A battery unit with a high-water alarm typically runs six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.
