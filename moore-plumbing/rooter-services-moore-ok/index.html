    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Moore

    Standard drain snaking in Moore, OK usually costs $150 to $500 depending on where the clog sits and how easy it is to reach. Main line root cutting and hydro jetting can reach $400 to $1,000 or more. A camera inspection adds to the bill. You get a firm price after the tech looks at the drain and before any cable goes in.

    Our trucks carry Spartan and RIDGID machines, plus jetters sized for residential and commercial lines. Both brands hold up to daily use. Several sizes of cable and cutter ride on each truck, so the tech matches the tool to the pipe rather than using one setup everywhere.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. Cables can kink, whip and wrap around a wrist in a second. They also get stuck. A rental cable stuck in an old clay line can turn a $300 rooter call into a dig to retrieve it.

    Sewer pipes leak a little water vapor at the joints. Roots chase that moisture to the joint and work their way inside. Inside, they thicken fast. In Moore, clay pipe with a joint every 2 to 3 feet and older cast iron with oakum joints are the most common targets.

    Rooting is safe with a trained tech and the correct cable and blade. Badly cracked or collapsed pipe can be harmed by any cleaning method. Feel matters. If the cable tells us the pipe is damaged, we stop and send in a camera before going further.

    No permit is required for routine rooting. Installing a new cleanout, digging up a broken section or replacing the sewer lateral usually does require a plumbing permit and an inspection. Permits for repair work are on us.

    Drain snaking, also called augering, uses a flexible cable and cutting head to break up or pull out the clog. Hair, wipes and roots respond well to it. Jetting scours the whole pipe with high-pressure water and strips the grease, scale and sludge a cable slides past. Repeat clogs usually need jetting.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. Heavy main line root cutting or a long jetting run can take 3 to 6 hours. You get a time estimate first. After looking at the cleanout and hearing which drains are slow, the tech can narrow the estimate.
