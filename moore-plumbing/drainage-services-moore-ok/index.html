    Downspout and Roof Drain Solutions in Moore

    Gutters concentrate a lot of roof water into a few points, often right next to the foundation. Soil movement can separate sewer lines and water lines over time, so foundation shifts and plumbing problems often go together. We pipe downspouts underground in solid PVC to a pop-up emitter or dry well well away from the house, with a cleanout at the base so the line can be flushed. An inch of rain on a 1,000 square foot roof sheds roughly 600 gallons. That is a lot of water.

    Restaurant and Industrial Drain Service in Moore

    Restaurants and industrial buildings send heavier loads down their drains than homes do. Interceptors need regular pumping.     Channel and Trench Drains in Moore, OK

    Driveways and patios that slope toward the house can send runoff straight into a garage or a door threshold. A trench drain cut into the concrete stops it. The channel goes into a saw-cut in the slab with a traffic-rated grate and solid pipe to an outlet.

    Hydro-Jetting Services in Moore

    A hydro jetter sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scours the full pipe wall. Unlike a cable, the jet cleans the whole pipe wall, not just a hole through the clog. Grease lines benefit most. We camera the line first to make sure the pipe is sound enough to jet.

    Dry Well Installation in Moore, OK

    Where there is no place to discharge on the surface, a dry well lets water soak into deeper soil. Drainage depends on the soil. A perc check comes first, then the well gets sized for the area it serves and set away from the foundation and septic.

    Clearing a clog does not need a permit. Repairs to the pipe itself often do. Replacing a section of sewer, adding a cleanout, or connecting a new drain to the sewer generally requires a permit and an inspection, and we handle that paperwork and schedule the inspector as part of the job.

    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It is quick and cheap. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the whole pipe wall clean of grease, scale and roots, so it lasts longer but costs more and needs a pipe that is in good enough shape to take the pressure.

    A typical French drain or downspout system takes one to three days. Larger projects run longer. Projects with dry wells, long trench runs, or concrete that has to be saw-cut for a channel drain can take most of a week, especially if the ground is too wet to dig cleanly the first day.

    A single fixture drain usually takes about an hour. Plan on one to two hours for a main line. Camera work adds 30 to 60 minutes, and a buried or missing cleanout adds more.
