    What Goes Into a Sewer Replacement in Midwest City, OK

    Under-Slab Sewer Replacement in Midwest City

    Sewer pipe under a slab is the hardest kind to reach. There are two options. We can cut and remove a strip of floor inside the house and patch it after, or dig a tunnel beneath the foundation from the yard, and the right choice depends on the length of the run, the flooring, and what sits above the pipe.

    Trenchless Sewer Repair Options in Midwest City, OK

    Pipe bursting needs two pits. One sits at the house, the other at the far end of the run. A cone-shaped head is pulled through the old line, breaking it outward, while fused HDPE pipe follows right behind it, so the new line takes the same path without a trench. Driveways stay put.

    Traditional Trench Sewer Replacement in Midwest City

    An open trench gives full access to the pipe from the house to the main. A small excavator does most of it, with hand digging near other lines. The old pipe comes out, the trench bottom gets a bed of gravel or sand, and new 4-inch PVC goes in at a steady quarter inch of fall per foot, confirmed with a level on every length. Then the camera.

    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in Midwest City, OK

    Clay pipe came in short sections with a joint every two or three feet. That is a lot of joints for roots to find. Orangeburg is worse, since the tar-paper wall flattens slowly under the dirt above it, and both are good candidates for bursting because they break apart easily. Fast pulls.

    Residential Sewer Replacement in Midwest City, OK

    Most house laterals run 30 to 100 feet. Some are two feet down, some are eight. We measure both before quoting, check whether the run passes under a driveway, porch or tree, then pick trench or trenchless based on what is in the way, so the estimate you sign is the price you pay.

    Sewer Scope Inspections for Replacement in Midwest City

    A sewer camera on a push cable goes in through the cleanout. The footage shows each joint, crack and root intrusion. A small transmitter called a sonde sits behind the camera head, so we can find its exact spot and depth from the yard above, which keeps the digging small. Bring questions.

    Addressing Tree Root Intrusions in Midwest City, OK

    Roots find sewer lines by following moisture. They need only a tiny gap. Once inside, they grow into a mat that catches paper and grease, and a root cutter clears them for a season at most, because the opening is still there. New PVC with glued joints gives them no way in.

    Trenchless Pipe Bursting in Midwest City

    Bursting works best on clay, cast iron and Orangeburg. A hydraulic rig at the receiving pit pulls the bursting head through the old line, splitting it into the soil, while a continuous length of fused HDPE follows behind which leaves a single joint-free pipe in the ground. Roots cannot get into a joint that does not exist.
