    PEX is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, resists scale and corrosion and installs faster than copper. Faster install means lower labor cost.     Type L copper appeals to some homeowners for its long history and rigid feel. We solder or press each joint. Since copper is pricier and slower to install, we walk through cost, water conditions and your plans for the house before you decide.

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. Check the heater first. You may also see it under sinks, near the main shutoff or coming through the garage wall, and we trace runs through the attic to confirm how much of the house it serves.

    Why Polybutylene Fails in Muskogee

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. The inner wall of the pipe and fittings slowly turns brittle, and small cracks grow until the pipe splits. Homeowners around Muskogee with hard water often add a softener to extend the life of water heaters and appliances. Heat makes it worse. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.
