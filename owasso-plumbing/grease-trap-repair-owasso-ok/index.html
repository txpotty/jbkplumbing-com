    Why Choose JBK Plumbing for Grease Trap Repair in Owasso, OK?

    FOG, short for fats, oils and grease, is the reason the trap exists. Once it gets past the trap, it hardens in the sewer. Most cities run a pretreatment program that inspects grease traps on a schedule and fines kitchens whose traps are damaged, missing parts or overdue for pumping. A working trap keeps you off that list.

    • Quality Parts, Manufacturer parts or commercial-grade materials that hold up to grease and hot water.

    Clogged Grease Trap Repair in Owasso

    Hardened grease in the inlet tee is the usual culprit when a trap backs up. We pump the trap down, then jet the inlet line with a 4,000 PSI unit until water runs free. Solids packed against the baffle come out by hand, with a scoop and a bucket. Then we look for the reason it clogged, such as a dishwasher dumping too much hot water at once or a missing sink strainer upstream. Repeat clogs nearly always have a cause.

    Grease Trap Drain Line Repair in Owasso

    Sometimes the trap is not the problem. Lines between the sinks and the trap clog first, because that is where hot water cools. We put a camera in from the nearest cleanout and record the run, looking for sags, offset joints, roots in clay sections downstream, or a belly where grease collects every week. Jetting a belly buys time, but only regrading the pipe fixes it.

    Damaged Baffle and Lid Repair in Owasso, OK

    Lids crack when someone sets a stockpot on them or a dolly rolls across a floor-mounted unit. Once cracked, it leaks smell out and lets mop water into the trap. We replace the lid and gasket with the manufacturer part, and on in-floor units we check that the lid sits flush so nobody catches a toe or a cart wheel on it. Baffle damage often shows up at the same visit.

    Grease Trap Odor Control & Repair in Owasso

    A sewer smell near the trap means gas is getting out somewhere it should not. The usual suspects are a torn lid gasket or a floor drain trap that has dried out. We check venting on the fixture side too, because a sink with no vent can siphon its own trap seal and let sewer gas back into the kitchen. Pumping the trap helps for a week, but it will not fix a gasket that no longer seals.
