    Pressure Switch Replacement in Owasso

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Pitted contacts, a clogged sensing tube and ant nests are the usual reasons one fails. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. This is among the least expensive well repairs.

    Constant Pressure System Repair in Owasso, OK

    Variable speed drives like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by changing motor speed. A fault code on the drive points us in the right direction. We troubleshoot the drive, the pressure sensor and the wiring, then repair or replace the failed part and verify the setpoint. These need a smaller tank.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Owasso, OK

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. That rapid cycling burns out pumps early. The air charge gets checked and the bladder inspected, then the tank is recharged or replaced so the pump runs a full minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Owasso

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. No water, low pressure or a pump that hums without moving water are the usual signs one has failed. Testing starts at the surface. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Owasso, OK

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Storm-related power outages can stop sump pumps and well pumps, so a standby power plan is worth having. Surges from lightning are the usual cause of control box failures. After testing the capacitor, relay and overload, we replace the bad part and suggest a surge arrestor if the box lacks one. Arrestors are cheap.

    Jet Pump Repair in Owasso, OK

Our crew in Owasso restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for leaks that let air in. That matters most where water levels rise and fall through the year, since a dropping level can starve the pump and cost it prime.
