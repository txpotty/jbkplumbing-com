    PEX for Water Line Replacement in Norman

    Your service line runs from the meter or well to the house, often a foot and a half to three feet underground. Because PEX comes on a long coil, the whole run from the meter to the foundation can go in with zero buried fittings. Most homes sit on concrete slabs, so leaks under the floor often call for slab leak detection before repair. We bed it in sand. Sharp rock against plastic pipe is how a new line fails early.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Norman

    PEX-B is stiffer, is cheaper by the roll, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. Since a crimp is only as good as the tool that made it, every ring gets a go/no-go gauge check, and any ring that fails gets cut off and redone. That takes seconds. Skipping it is how leaks start.

    PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Norman, OK

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A bends the easiest and uses expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and uses crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C handles more like B. All three are code-listed for drinking water. Pipe, fittings and tools come from the same maker on our jobs, which keeps the warranty valid.

    PEX-A Expansion Fitting Systems in Norman, OK

    With PEX-A, the tech slips a ring over the pipe end and stretches both with a battery tool. The fitting goes in while the plastic is still expanded, and the material's memory pulls it back down around the barb, sealing harder over the first minute. Cold weather slows it. Joints get time to set before we pressurize.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Norman

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. We stretch the pipe and ring, seat the fitting, and within seconds the plastic shrinks back around the barb into a tight seal. There is no crimp gauge to check. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Norman, OK

    Gray polybutylene went into millions of homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorine in treated water attacks the plastic and the acetal fittings from the inside, so it can look fine from outside right up to the day a joint lets go. Insurers ask about it. Swapping it for PEX can help with insurance and with resale.
