    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Norman

    The cost for sewage ejector pump installation in Norman typically ranges from $2,500 to $5,000. This price can vary depending on the pump type, basin size, complexity of the plumbing and electrical work and whether an existing system needs to be removed. We provide a detailed quote after assessing your project.

    A standard sewage ejector pump installation usually takes one to two days. This includes excavating the pit, setting the basin and pump, connecting the plumbing and completing the electrical hookups. More complex installations, like those requiring significant trenching or electrical panel upgrades, may take longer.

    A well-maintained sewage ejector pump typically has a lifespan of 7 to 10 years, though some higher-quality units can last longer. Factors like frequency of use, quality of installation and the type of waste being pumped can affect its longevity. Regular maintenance can help extend its operating life.

    JBK Plumbing offers a solid warranty on all sewage ejector pump installations, covering both parts and labor. The warranty period can vary based on the pump manufacturer's warranty and our service guarantee. We will provide full details on your coverage before starting any work.

    Signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include unusual noises from the pump or pit, frequent cycling on and off, slow drainage from basement fixtures or a sewage odor coming from the area. If you notice any of these issues, it is best to have a professional check your system to prevent a complete failure and costly backups.

    We primarily install high-quality, reputable brands such as Liberty Pumps and Zoeller. These manufacturers are known for their durability, reliability and efficient performance, ensuring a long-lasting solution for your home. We can discuss the best brand and model for your needs.

    We recommend having your sewage ejector pump inspected annually, especially if it is in a high-use area. Regular inspections can identify potential issues early, extend the life of your pump and prevent unexpected failures and costly backups. This is especially important for older units.
