    Reduced Pressure Zone Assembly Installation in Norman, OK

    RPZ assemblies offer the highest level of protection and are required for high hazard connections like commercial kitchens, medical facilities, chemical injection and car washes. They include two independent check valves with a relief valve that dumps water if either check fails. Because the relief valve can discharge, we plan for drainage and install the assembly above grade where it can be tested. We carry trusted brands like Watts, Febco, Wilkins and Apollo.

    Pressure Vacuum Breaker (PVB) Installation in Norman, OK

    For low-hazard applications, such as lawn irrigation systems in Norman, OK, a pressure vacuum breaker, or PVB, is a popular and cost-effective backflow prevention device. PVBs protect against back-siphonage by letting air into the system when pressure drops. Our certified plumbers expertly install PVBs above the highest point of downstream piping, as code requires, to maintain effective protection. We also check for proper clearance and drainage.

    Reduced Pressure Zone (RPZ) Device Installation in Norman, OK

    For high-hazard applications in Norman, OK where toxic substances could contaminate the water supply, a reduced pressure zone, or RPZ, device is often required. RPZ backflow preventers offer the highest level of protection, using a relief valve to discharge water if the pressure drops.     Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer Installation in Norman

    The cost for backflow preventer installation in Norman, OK can range from about $300 to $1,500, depending on the type of device required, such as a DCVA or an RPZ, the complexity of the installation and any changes needed to existing plumbing.     Backflow preventers are installed at cross-connection points where non-potable water could enter the potable water supply. Common locations include irrigation system connections, fire sprinkler lines, boiler systems and connections to auxiliary water supplies, usually just after the main water meter or at the point of hazard.

    Most residential installs take two to four hours including testing. Large commercial assemblies can take a full day, especially if piping must be reconfigured.

    Many jurisdictions require a permit for new backflow assemblies, especially on commercial properties. We check local rules and pull the permit when required.
