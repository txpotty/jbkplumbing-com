    Most homes never need scheduled drain cleaning. A slow drain is the signal. Homes with root-prone sewers, old cast iron, or a kitchen that sends a lot of grease down the drain do better with a cleaning every 12 to 24 months, timed before the line slows to a crawl.

    Simple downspout extensions are a reasonable DIY project. French drains are harder. They need proper slope and a legal outlet, and digging without an 811 locate is risky.

    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It is quick and cheap.     They solve different problems. French drains handle water moving through soil. A channel drain catches surface water sheeting across concrete, like a driveway that slopes toward the garage, and many yards need one of each because the water arrives both ways during a hard storm.

    Store-bought cleaners rarely clear a real clog. They can make things worse. They sit in the trap, can damage pipe and joints, and leave caustic liquid for the plumber. A hand auger is the better tool.
