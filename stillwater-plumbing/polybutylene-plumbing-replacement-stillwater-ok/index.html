    Copper Replacement Option in Stillwater

    Type L copper appeals to some homeowners for its long history and rigid feel. We solder or press each joint. Copper costs more than PEX and takes a little longer to install, so we help you weigh the cost, your water conditions and how long you plan to stay in the house.

    Full Home Repiping with PEX in Stillwater

    Most poly replacements we do use PEX-A, a flexible tubing that runs long distances with few fittings. For slab houses, new PEX-A runs through the attic and drops down interior walls, so none of it sits under the floor. It also tolerates freezing better. Expansion fittings keep the bore full size, which helps pressure at the last fixture on the line.

    Copper Pipe Replacement in Stillwater

    Copper is a solid alternative to PEX for poly replacement. It is rigid, handles heat well and has decades of history in homes. Plan on a higher price. We review your water and any history of copper pinholes nearby before recommending it over PEX.

    Why Polybutylene Fails in Stillwater

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. Over the years, the pipe and its acetal fittings get brittle from the inside, then crack along the inner wall until one splits under normal pressure. Homeowners around Stillwater with hard water often add a softener to protect water heaters and appliances. Heat makes it worse. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.
