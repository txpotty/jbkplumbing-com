    Water Heater Repair and Replacement in Stillwater, OK

    Thermocouples, gas valves, elements and relief valves are the common water heater repairs.     Pipe Repair and Repipes in Stillwater, OK

    Pinhole leaks and rust-colored water from the tap usually point to supply pipe near the end of its working life in an older house. A single leak gets a clean copper splice or a PEX transition. More than one leak usually means repiping. A pipe split by a hard freeze gets cut out and replaced, then pressure-tested.

    Gas Lines for New Appliances in Stillwater, OK

    Switching from an electric range to gas means a new line and a shutoff. Pipe size is figured from the BTU load, run it in black iron or CSST, bond it where the code requires and pressure-test before the gas company or inspector signs off on the work. Then we light it and check the flame.

    Pressure Regulators and Shutoff Valves in Stillwater, OK

    Water pressure over 80 psi wears out hoses and valves early. A gauge on the hose bib tells us your static pressure in a minute, and if it reads high we install or replace a pressure reducing valve and set it in the 50 to 70 psi range most fixtures are rated for. Seized main shutoffs get replaced too.

    Drain Cleaning and Sewer Camera Service in Stillwater

    Wipes are not flushable, whatever the package says. Kitchen grease, hair and wipes cause most slow drains, and for a main line backup we cable it open, then run a camera to look for roots, offset joints or a belly where water sits in the pipe. You get the video.

    Faucet, Toilet and Shower Valve Repair in Stillwater, OK

    Hard water chews through faucet cartridges and toilet fill valves. Shower valves get rebuilt, cartridges replaced and tub spout leaks sealed, and if a fixture is too corroded to save, we install the replacement and haul the old one away so it is not sitting in your garage. A slow drip still adds up on the bill.

    Slab Leak Detection and Repair in Stillwater

    Slab leaks hide under concrete until something gives them away. Soil movement can separate sewer lines and water lines over time, so foundation shifts and plumbing problems often go together. We locate them with acoustic listening gear and a thermal camera, so the only concrete we break is the small patch right over the leak, not half the kitchen floor. Then you pick the fix. Some leaks get a spot repair, others a reroute overhead that skips the slab entirely.

    Repiping and Supply Line Replacement in Stillwater

    Polybutylene, the gray plastic pipe used for years in home construction, fails from the inside without warning. We replace it, along with corroded galvanized and pinholed copper, using PEX or copper, routing new lines through the attic and walls so the slab stays intact and the house keeps water each night. We patch the access holes, ready for paint.
