    Manifold and Home-Run PEX Layouts in Bartlesville, OK

    Think of a manifold as a breaker panel for water. Each fixture gets its own line from a central header, and each line gets its own valve, so you can shut off one toilet without killing water to the kitchen. It uses more pipe. The trade-off is almost no fittings hidden in the walls and steadier pressure when two showers run at once.

    Emergency PEX Pipe Repair & Installation in Bartlesville, OK

    PEX rarely fails on its own. When it does, the cause is usually a nail or screw driven through it, a rodent chewing on an attic run, or a fitting that was never crimped all the way.     PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Bartlesville

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Either one lasts for decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.

    PEX for Remodels & Additions in Bartlesville, OK

    Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel is the cheapest time to replace old supply lines. A far-end bathroom may need a three-quarter inch feed to hold pressure.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Bartlesville

    PEX-B is stiffer, is cheaper by the roll, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. A crimp joint is only as good as the tool, so we check every ring with a go/no-go gauge, and a ring that does not pass gets cut out and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it causes most crimp leaks.
