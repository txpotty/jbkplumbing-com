    Electric Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Shawnee, OK

    Electric tankless water heaters skip the gas line entirely, but a whole-house model can need 120 to 160 amps on two to four double-pole breakers. Many panels cannot spare that. Panel size and wire gauge get checked first.

    Whole-House Electric Tankless Replacement in Shawnee

    Whole-house electric tankless performance drops when the incoming water is cold, since the unit has to raise the temperature further at the same amperage. We replace Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart units. A heat pump water heater is sometimes the better fit, and we will tell you.

    Tank to Tankless Conversion in Shawnee, OK

    Converting from a tank to tankless takes a full day in most homes. Expect a bigger gas line, a new vent run and a wall-mounted spot for the heater. The old flue gets capped.

    Commercial Tankless Replacement in Shawnee

    Cascade controls on a commercial rack decide which unit fires first, and after one unit is replaced they need rebalancing. Otherwise one unit does all the work. We also set a descaling schedule.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Shawnee, OK

    Commercial tankless setups in restaurants and offices often use two or more units on a manifold, with a controller that splits the load. One bad unit can come out while the others keep running. Hand sinks and dish stations have to hit the temperatures health code requires.

    Like-for-Like Tankless Swaps in Shawnee

    A cracked heat exchanger or a failed control board on an older tankless is usually not worth repairing past the ten-year mark. We match the new unit to your gas line, vent type and mounting spot whenever we can. Most swaps take four to six hours.

    Condensing Tankless Replacement in Shawnee

    Condensing tankless units like the Navien NPE or Rinnai Sensei pass 90 percent efficiency by pulling extra heat out of the exhaust. They vent through PVC. Acidic condensate goes through a neutralizer and then to an approved drain. Summer heat shrinks clay soil around foundations, which can shift slabs and stress the plumbing beneath them. Showers and laundry run all year, so the gas savings do too.
