    Flow Sensor and Thermistor Repair in Shawnee

    Tankless heaters start when the flow sensor sees about half a gallon a minute, then trim the burner using thermistor readings. A dirty inlet screen or a failing sensor shows up as no hot water, temperature swings or a unit that drops out mid-shower. We pull the screen, read sensor values in the service menu and compare thermistor resistance to the chart. Usually under an hour.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Repair in Shawnee, OK

    Rinnai units are common, and most of their trouble starts with scale. The LC code is the unit's own warning that the exchanger needs a flush. We also see dirty flame rods behind a code 11 or 12 and tired combustion fans behind a code 61, and we test every one against Rinnai's published values. Parts come from the factory list.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Repair in Shawnee

    Restaurants and laundromats in Shawnee often run two to six tankless units on a common manifold. When one unit drops out, the rest carry the load and scale up sooner, so we check the cascade controller, the isolation valves on each heater and the water quality coming in. A down kitchen loses money. We schedule around service hours when we can.

    Tankless Flush and Scale Removal in Shawnee

    Lukewarm water at high flow and a unit that runs loud are two early signs of scale. Most tankless installs include isolation valves with purge ports for exactly this job, and if yours does not, we can add them during the visit so future flushing takes less time. White vinegar will do in a pinch. Commercial descaler works faster.

    Noritz Tankless Water Heater Repair in Shawnee, OK

    Noritz heaters hold up well, but their flow sensors and vent connections still wear out. A code 90 points to combustion trouble, often a dirty fan or a restricted intake. We clean the fan and burner, check the vent joints for gaps, and test the flow sensor's output against the spec sheet. Gaskets get replaced if they leak.

    Ignition Failure Repair in Shawnee, OK

    No ignition codes have four usual causes: a dirty flame rod, a bad igniter, low gas pressure or a blocked intake. Undersized gas pipe is the one people miss. A tankless burner can need 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, and the half-inch line that fed a 40,000 BTU tank often cannot carry that, which is why it fires at low flow and quits when two showers are on.

    Tankless Repair vs Replacement Cost in Shawnee, OK

    Descaling usually runs $175 to $350, while a sensor or igniter repair tends to run $250 to $600. A heat exchanger out of warranty can cost $800 to $1,500 in parts and labor. A new unit costs a lot more. Installed, a new gas tankless typically runs $3,000 to $5,500, so on a unit past 15 years with a failed exchanger we usually lean toward replacing.

    Igniter & Flame Rod Repair in Shawnee, OK

    Gas tankless heaters light with a hot surface or spark igniter, then prove flame with a flame rod. A rod coated in oxide reads low on a microamp meter and the board shuts the gas valve after a few seconds. We clean or replace the rod, look for a cracked igniter or a lazy spark, and confirm the gas valve opens on call. Five-minute fix sometimes.
