    Why a Sewer Camera Inspection Belongs on Every Oklahoma Home Inspection

    By Andrew Blake · August 13, 2026 · 5 min read

    What a Standard Home Inspection Misses

    A general home inspector runs the faucets and flushes the toilets, and that is about where the drain check ends. Nobody looks at the sewer lateral, which runs from the house out to the main or the septic tank. Of all the pipe on the lot, it costs the most to replace. Sellers rarely know its condition.

    Our crews at JBK Plumbing inspect lines for buyers across Oklahoma, and a camera inspection usually takes well under an hour. The fee is small next to a replacement. Vacant houses are the riskiest, because a line can sit broken for months with nobody flushing to reveal it.

    Most homes sit on concrete slabs, so leaks under the floor often call for slab leak detection before repair.

    Older laterals were often clay tile or cast iron, and years of shifting soil and growing roots break both of them down. Roots get in where clay joints pull apart. Cast iron corrodes along the bottom until the sewage is flowing over soil, not pipe. Neither shows up from inside the house until there is a backup.

    What the Camera Shows

    The camera goes in through a cleanout or a pulled toilet and travels the full length of the line out to the main. A transmitter in the camera head lets us mark the location and depth of anything we find from the surface. We give you the video. Here is what we look for.

    • Root intrusion at joints
    • Bellies, or sags, that hold standing water
    • Cracks, breaks and offset joints
    • Corroded or collapsed cast iron
    • Orangeburg pipe that has started to flatten
    • Grease buildup or debris from a past backup

    Septic systems are common in rural Oklahoma, and aerobic systems with spray heads are a familiar sight on many properties.

    On a septic property, the camera also shows the line from the house to the tank. That run gets missed often. A sag or root mass near the inlet baffle is common, and a pump-out alone will not do anything to fix it. Ask the seller for the last pump-out date and any drain field records. A camera does not test the drain field itself, so a septic inspection is a separate step.

    Inspection Cost Versus Repair Cost

    Sewer line repairs range from a single spot repair to a full lateral replacement under the driveway. A spot repair might be a day of work, while a full replacement under concrete can take a week and a lot of restoration. After closing, the bill belongs to you. Before closing, it is a negotiating point.

    Buyers use the video in a few ways. Some negotiate a credit or a lower price at closing. Others ask the seller to have the repair done by a licensed plumber with a permit before closing, then rerun the camera to confirm it. The video is evidence.

    Scheduling an Inspection

    Book the camera inspection during your inspection period, not after. The inspection window in most contracts is short, often 7 to 10 days, so get on the schedule early. You can reach JBK Plumbing at (405) 507-8396. It helps to have the address and the build year when you call. Most inspections get scheduled within two days, and the recording goes to you and your agent that afternoon.
